History of Las Nieves

"The Snows" - A legacy of faith, peace, and cultural heritage

Origin of the Name

Las Nieves (Spanish for "The Snows") got its name from Spanish Jesuit Missionary Father Saturnino Urios. Upon arriving in the area in April 1877, he and his companions were struck by the unusually cold conditions. The valley was enveloped in thick fog and intense chill, giving the impression of falling snow. The scene reminded Father Urios of his hometown in Játiva, Spain, prompting them to exclaim, "¡La nieve! ¡La nieve!" ("The snow! The snow!").

Early History

The first traders to enter Las Nieves were natives of Butuan City. They used to travel along the Agusan River in wooden dug-out boats or baoto using a long paddle called ga-o. The Manobo and Higaonon tribes, including the Jovellar, Tepnage, Bayan, Legis, Taga-ang and Kulisan families, were the first dominant families in the area. Respected warriors, or bagani, such as Dagohoy and Kulisan were considered influential leaders.

The Manobo and Higaonon peoples traditionally used bags made from woven abaca fibers, known as camuyot, to carry their belongings. Their customary entertainment included traditional war dances like kalasag, as well as ceremonial dances like binaylan, inamo, and binanog. These performances were often accompanied by the agong, a musical instrument believed to have been made of pure gold.

The Jesuit Mission

In 1875, on orders from the Bishop of Cebu, a group of Spanish Jesuit Missionaries disembarked at the Port of Butuan. In February 1877, Father Saturnino Urios, with two other Jesuit Missionaries, Father Ramon Pamies and Father Jose Casellas, announced the beginning of their mission to preach the gospel to the natives.

In April 1877, Father Urios reported to his superior in Manila the successful founding of five villages along the Agusan River. The villages were Las Nieves, Remedios, Esperanza, Guadalupe and San Luis.

Las Nieves, originally named Pinana-an (a place for hunting), was the area that Father Urios first founded. The natives or Manobos used to catch wild pigs, birds, and fishes with their bows and arrows or panâ here.

Foundation as a Municipality

Eufronio Rosales was first elected mayor in 1955. He later became the first municipal mayor following the creation of the Municipality of Las Nieves through a Republic Act on July 2, 1962. The municipality was formally separated from Esperanza on June 25, 1963, by virtue of Executive Order No. 42 issued by President Diosdado Macapagal.

Patron Saint

In honor of their patroness, the Nuestra Señora de Las Nieves (Our Lady of the Snow), Father Urios named this place Las Nieves.
